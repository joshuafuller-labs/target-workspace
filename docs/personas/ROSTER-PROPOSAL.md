# Persona Roster — Proposal (pending sign-off)

Proposed roster of eight primary personas, two per customer segment. Each persona will get a full profile (background, goals, pains, context, tools today, success criteria) once this roster is approved.

Names are composite, not literal. Ranks/grades/agencies are illustrative.

## DoD Tactical

### 1. SSG Marcus "Diaz" Reyes — SOF Intelligence NCO (35F-equivalent in a SOF unit)
- Mid-30s, TS/SCI, ~12 years in.
- Lives in mission planning and target deconfliction.
- Runs ATAK + WinTAK during ops.
- Core pain: stitching SIGINT, GEOINT, HUMINT cues into actionable target packages on a clock; lots of cross-tool copy/paste.

### 2. Captain Lena Park, USAF — JTAC
- Late-20s, deployed.
- Owns CAS coordination from the ground side.
- Core pain: target nominations arrive from drone feed, eyes-on, and intel cell in different formats; needs to validate and push CoT to ATAK fast under fire.

## DoD Operational

### 3. MAJ Brian Holloway — Battle Captain, BCT TOC
- Mid-30s, infantry or aviation, mid-rotation.
- Watch officer running the brigade TOC across 12-hour shifts.
- Core pain: targets queued by multiple cells (intel, fires, ISR) with no single visible workflow. SIPR chat and butcher paper are the system today.

### 4. Asha Mehta, GS-13 — All-source intel cell, JSOC J2 (or equivalent targeting cell)
- Late-30s, ex-Army WO1, now civilian targeting analyst.
- Owns F3EAD target packages end-to-end.
- Core pain: F3EAD lives in PowerPoint, Excel, and SharePoint; no kanban-style view of where each target sits in the cycle, no audit trail of who moved what when.

## Federal LE / DHS

### 5. SA Tomás Garcia — HSI counter-narcotics surveillance squad
- Mid-30s.
- Runs surveillance ops on smuggling targets.
- ATAK on duty phone in the truck.
- Core pain: tips, fixed cameras, license plate readers, and AI analytics generate a flood of leads; no shared queue with accept/reject workflow; analysts and field agents work from different lists.

### 6. Renee Calderón — CBP Air Interdiction Agent (AMO, P-3/MEA platform)
- Late-20s.
- Tracks suspected smuggling tracks via radar, IR sensors, and tip-line cues.
- Core pain: target hand-off from sensor operator to ground-based agents is verbal/radio; nothing persistent shows status of each cue across shift changes.

## State/Local LE & Public Safety

### 7. Lieutenant Mike O'Connor — SWAT Commander, mid-sized city PD
- Mid-40s.
- Runs SWAT ops: high-risk warrants, barricaded subjects, active shooter response.
- Core pain: target package built in PowerPoint, briefed verbally, no live tracking of suspect/vehicle/location during the op; situational awareness lives in radio chatter.

### 8. Diana Chen — Search and Rescue Coordinator, county Office of Emergency Management
- Mid-30s.
- Coordinates volunteer SAR teams and aviation assets during missing-person and disaster events.
- Core pain: missing-persons reports, sighting tips, drone OSINT, and AI-detected imagery cues arrive faster than the team can triage; no single board showing "lead -> assigned -> searched -> cleared."

## Anti-personas (explicitly NOT designed for)

- General public / citizen-side mapping users (e.g., Citizen app audience)
- Battlefield management users on bespoke systems with no CoT/TAK interface
- Pure mission planners with no real-time operations role (deliberate planning tools serve them better)
- Casual ATAK hobbyists and tinkerers without an operational workflow

## What I'd want you to weigh in on

1. Are these the right eight? Any swaps?
2. Any segments under- or over-weighted? (Currently 2 each.)
3. Any named persona that doesn't ring true — wrong rank, wrong role, wrong agency?
4. Any anti-persona missing? (Anti-personas are surprisingly load-bearing for scope.)
5. Should we also have a **buyer persona** distinct from end-user personas? (PEO, program manager, agency CIO, sheriff — the people who sign the check.)
